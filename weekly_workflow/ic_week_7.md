This systematic review examined 85 studies on Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment (DDA) in video games, analyzing machine-learning methods, player modeling techniques, player-state data, game genres, and evaluation approaches. The findings show that DDA generally improves player enjoyment, engagement, immersion, motivation, and flow, with increasing use of machine learning and player modeling to personalize gameplay. The review highlights the importance of gameplay and psychophysiological data for adapting difficulty and identifies research gaps in cognitive modeling, biological constraints, multiplayer environments, and complex game genres. Overall, the study provides a comprehensive framework to guide researchers and developers in designing more effective, personalized DDA systems.
